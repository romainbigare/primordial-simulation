## What that means, in order of priority
1. **One look at every zoom.** What a far view shows as one tone is the mean of what the near view draws there: the same numbers, the same palette, near detail fading in over the far tone and never replacing it with another. Prove it: pairs of pictures of the same place and moment at each scale bar (20 km, 2 km, 200 m, 20 m, 2 m) with the mean colour of each, on dry land, forest, open land, shore and snow. The mean colour may not jump between neighbouring zoom steps.
2. **No grid, no lattice, no tile seams.** The faults in the owner's pictures: crowns as a dark maze of straight strokes at 20 m; green and brown in square blocks at 50 m; dark smears of fine horizontal and vertical lines and thin straight lines at tile borders at 2 m; green over rock in straight lines at 10 m. Find the cause of each (do not paint over it) and mend it.
3. **Habitats that read at a glance and reward looking closer.** Each kind of ground and cover gets its own texture, relief and detail, keyed to the numbers: dry open land (cured straw grass in the dry season, green in the wet, tufts, bare patches, stones, cracked ground where it is driest); forest (crowns of many forms and sizes, gaps, fallen trunks, undergrowth); leafless dry-season woods (bare branches over litter); cold open land (low mats, stones, snow patches); shores and river beds (gravel bars, sand, wet edges, reeds where the ground is soaked); rock and scree on steep high ground; snow and ice with relief.
4. **Plants that look like what they are.** A bush is not a small tree; a herb is not a bush; a tall slow tree, a tall fast one, a dry woody one and a wet-ground one differ in form and tone because their numbers differ (height, crown width and depth, leaf per crown, walls, leaflessness, youth). Flowers and seed heads where a kind is putting its mass into seeds, in its season.
5. **Variation inside a kind.** Plants of one kind differ a little in form, size, tone, lean and age, spread around the kind's own numbers, never changing what the square holds in sum.
6. **Free detail is welcome**: boulders, logs, tufts, stones in a stream, driftwood. It is keyed to the place (a hash of world position), so it does not move between frames or zoom levels, and it follows the ground (no boulder on open water, no log on a snowfield without trees near).
