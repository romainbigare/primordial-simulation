# Decisions of the speed work

The owner asked the architect to take every decision of this work and to log it here. Each row: what was decided,
why, and what to change if the owner disagrees. The runs behind a row are in `docs/history.md` (section 5) and in
`measures.md`.

| # | Step | Decision | Why | If you disagree |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| S1 | all | Ideas A to E of the speed list are built; group F (life as a field, slow stores sped up, climate on repeat, a GPU, a learned shortcut, threads that may change the history) waits | The owner's choice (24 October 2026) | — |
| S2 | all | The plan, the decisions and the measures live in `docs/speed-post-foundation/`; the closing watch report goes in `docs/reports/speed.md` | The owner asked for this folder; `CLAUDE.md` 4.5 keeps watch reports in `docs/reports/` | Move the report into this folder |
| S3 | D2.1 | D2 is built on the branch `speed/d2`, each sub-step committed and pushed there; the D2.1 commit on that branch raises the core ceiling to 9,700 code lines; the branch merges into `main` only when D2.5 shows its gain on the benches; if D2 is killed, the branch is not merged and the ceiling stays 9,000. Before D2.1 every step lives under 9,000: B and C together add no net line (each step gives its estimate), every other step pays for its own lines. E has no extra allowance: it lives under 9,700 and simplifies as much as it adds | Decision 42 of the foundation keeps 700 lines for threads over bodies "for a design that pays"; the lines are only spent where the gain is shown, and a killed D2 leaves `main` as it was | Keep 9,000 and simplify more |
| S4 | D2 | The bodies run on threads by **domains with an outbox**, not by a pure "read, then write" step over all bodies | Inside a domain the bodies run in order, each seeing what the one before took, so no take can overdraw a node and no update must run twice; only what crosses a domain's edge waits for the batch's end. A pure read-then-write step would let two bodies take the same water, and fixing that needs a second run of the updates | A read-then-write step with a second pass for the nodes that were overdrawn |
| S5 | A4 | A faster machine is not built in code; the report says what a newer CPU would give | It is the owner's money, not the code's | — |
| S6 | B1, B3 | "Plants as size classes" (B3 of the list) is built as one node per kind per cell whose members have many sizes (B1, for nodes without a brain only, S29), with its young part and adult part as two layers of light (the perfect plasticity approximation, Purves et al. 2008), not as several fixed classes | Several classes would be more bodies, not fewer; two layers keep the shade by height that B1 would else lose | Fixed size classes per kind (more bodies, finer shade) |
| S7 | B2 | Past `crowd` at a month's start, the most alike pair of one lineage in a cell merges when the merged node's pooled spread stays within its cap (`body::most`) on every gene; unlike nodes beyond that stay apart. Every merge path (`join_alike`, `merge_closest`, the kin joins of `copy`, `spread::part_acts`) asks one predicate, `nodes::may_join`, built in B2 with no other change of behaviour. If the milder rule does not bound the bodies, B2 reports with numbers; a stronger rule needs a new decision | A merge that keeps the pooled spread under the cap destroys no difference beyond what one node can hold (the "similar states" of handover 7.1); one predicate is one place for the idea of who may join whom | Merge nodes of one lineage whatever their genes (bounds the bodies, loses differences) |
| S8 | C3 (D2.2) | `batch_days` is 5, the shortest interval (`MIN_INTERVAL`). A body's interval is a whole number of batches: its `INTERVAL` gene's days rounded to the nearest multiple of `batch_days`, at least one batch; `node_days` must be a multiple of `batch_days` (it becomes the nearest multiple). `Config::validate` refuses a `batch_days` that does not divide `phys_days` and the month, and a `node_days` that is not a multiple of it. A body placed during a batch is first due at the next batch's start or later | Every due day is then a batch's start: no body runs early, every law that reads `Body::interval` reads the real gap, and there is no jitter to correct. Short intervals lose resolution (6 and 7 days both become 5); the `batch_days` equivalence test checks that this changes no key number past its noise | Keep the gene's own days and run bodies up to 4 days early (a bias in every law that reads the interval) |
| S9 | D1 | The physics of the next step does not run beside the bodies unless the measure of D1 (b) finds more than 25% of a year with three cores idle. If it does: bodies take only from their cell's bodies' node, which the air's step fills for them from what they took at their last update (as their heat is filled today, `survey::Draw`), and the bodies' nodes are kept twice so the physics fills one while the bodies take from the other | Once D2 and D3 put both stages on all cores, running them side by side gains only their idle time. Running them side by side on today's nodes would let a body take what the physics has just moved away, an overdraw | Build the double bodies' node now |
| S10 | D2.1 | A body's id is its domain of birth and that domain's count of births; it never changes. The random draws are keyed by it | Ids must stay stable when a body moves between domains (memory of individuals, `Body::known`); a slot is no longer one number for the whole world | Keep a global slot table, filled only in the serial phase |
| S11 | D2.1 | Each body with a brain keeps its own born rows; `Life::genomes` and its counts go | Shared rows with counts are a registry that every domain would write; bodies with a brain are few, so the memory cost is small | Keep the shared rows, with their counts settled in the serial phase |
| S12 | C1 | Dormant sleep is built only if at least 5% of the updates of bodies without a brain on the benches find the body nearly inactive | Under that it saves too little for its lines | Build it anyway |
| S13 | A1 | The benches are `island22` (seed 1, year 300) and `globe36` (seed 1, globe 36 × 18, year 200), kept outside the repository and remade by `scripts/bench.sh --remake` | A settled island and a settled globe are the two shapes the time budget is about; snapshots are not study files for the repository | Other sizes or years |
| S14 | D2 | Each batch starts with a face of every body (id, cell, members, lineage, visible traits, signal, well-being, room root and closedness; about 64 to 80 bytes) and, for every cell, the list of the ids in it. Perception reads only faces and those lists, whether the other body is in the same domain or not; `Choice::seen` holds ids, not slots. Taking and giving (same cell, so same domain) resolve the id in the domain and act on the live body; if it is gone, the act fails as today. Watching reads the other's whole brain rows: it always goes to the outbox and is applied in the serial phase on the live target, so no rows are copied into faces | One rule everywhere, so a cell at a domain's edge sees others as a cell inside one does; faces stay small | Read live faces inside a domain |
| S15 | E3 | The stage-by-stage path for bodies without a brain (E3, built only if a profile after D2 shows a stage of their updates repeating work worth at least 10% of the bodies' time) changes the order of the updates of a domain's bodies (stage by stage): level 3, not level 1 | The whole gain of E2 is to run each stage over all bodies; the bodies of a cell still take from their nodes in a fixed order, so the books hold | Keep the per-body order and gain less |
| S16 | all | Each closed step is committed to `main` and pushed, without a pull request; D2.1 to D2.5 alone are committed and pushed on the branch `speed/d2` (S3) | The owner's instruction for this work | Branches and pull requests |
| S17 | D2 | A domain is one air node and its cells (4 × 4 today) | Every body update touches its air node; the air node is the smallest group of cells whose store nodes no other group touches | Larger chunks (fewer, larger tasks; worse load balance) |
| S18 | all | The slow suite runs only at the end of each phase; each step runs the normal suite, the golden test, clippy, the wasm build and the benches | The owner asked to keep runs to a minimum until they are fast | — |
| S19 | all | Opus agents build B1, B3, C2, D2.1, D2.3 to D2.5, E1, E3 and the hot spot of `spread::part_acts`; Sonnet agents build A, the other hot spots of E2, B2, C1, D1, D2.2 (C3), D2.6, D3 and the docs; Haiku agents gather facts. Every phase and the big D2 sub-steps get an adversarial Opus review and an Opus fixer until the reviewer approves | The owner's instruction | — |
| S20 | all | The goal stays "10 times faster" (the owner's aim), but the plan says plainly that A to E likely give about 3 to 5 times overall: bodies about 8 to 15 times cheaper, the rest (Flow, Change, parts, tape) about 2 times. Reaching 10 times needs group F or new physics algorithms | The baseline (`measures.md`): the rest is 219 of 857 ms a year on the island and 338 of 1,347 ms on the globe, more than the whole 10-times budget (86 and 135 ms) | Build group F now |
| S21 | D3 | D3 is "the physics and the survey on threads": Flow's serial per-cell loops, Change's serial parts with a profile-driven look at `Reactor::react` (bit for bit where possible), and the survey's per-cell loops (as D2.6) | The rest of a year must shrink too (S20); Flow is 17 to 19% and Change 4 to 6% of a year | Threads for Flow only |
| S22 | D2 | Every relocation of a body or of members to another cell (`choose::go`, members that move in `spread::part_acts`, children who start in another cell) goes through the outbox and lands at the batch's end, inside the domain as well as across its edge. Until then the body stays in its old cell, so the rest of its update runs there, in its own domain. The outbox kinds, their order and their failure rules are those of plan 3.6 | One path: a cell at a domain's edge behaves like any other, and no update writes a cell of another domain (crossings, pushed energy, lanes, parts there) | Move at once inside a domain, through the outbox only across its edge (two paths) |
| S23 | D2 | A domain owns the parts of its cells as it owns its bodies, with stable part ids (domain of birth and a counter); `Part::held` keeps body ids. Body–part actions stay immediate inside the domain; only a structure carried across a domain's edge (in a relocation) and the monthly Bond step cross domains. `bond::step` runs serially over all domains, as today | A part lies in one cell, so in one domain, like the bodies that act on it | Keep parts global and send every part action to the outbox |
| S24 | D2 | A lineage made in the parallel phase gets a provisional id of its domain; the domain keeps its brain until the serial phase maps it to a global id (in domain order, then the order made) and rewrites the ids its bodies and its outbox hold. Lineage member counts and a lineage dying out are settled only in the serial phase. A test checks that the remap gives the same lineages with and without threads | `Life::brains` and `Life::members` are global registries; a fixed order of the remap keeps the history the same with or without threads | A lock on the registry (order would depend on the threads) |
| S25 | D2 | A litter that disperses to another cell is taken from its parent in the parallel phase (`give`) and goes to the outbox with no kin search. The serial phase does the kin search in the target cell and joins or places it. If nothing joins and the litter is less than one member, it goes back into its parent's brood if the parent lives (by id), else to the ground node of the cell its parent was in, as a move. The `Born` event is made when the litter lands. Level 3 | The kin search reads the nodes of the target cell, which may be another domain's. Today such a litter is not made at all (it waits in the brood); now it is made and may come back, so the books hold either way | Give up dispersal of fractional litters |
| S26 | D2 | The store view of a domain holds the Ground, Bodies and Bed nodes of every cell of the domain and its air node, and a per-domain delta of the Potential node. Every direct read of `w.store` in body code goes through the view. A debug check holds that a take from the view never passes what the view holds | Body code reads the Bed (anchor, dig, the skin's heat capacity) and the ground's heat (`World::thaw`) besides what `store::Moves` keeps today; a read past the view would read a node another thread may change | A view of the three nodes of one cell, as today (misses the Bed and the other cells under the air node) |
| S27 | C1 | Dormant sleep extends `clock::Sleep`, the one sleep and wake of the world, to body nodes: an alarm per dormant body, woken through `Sleep`'s wake when its cell's ground temperature and liquid would raise its activity past the threshold, or at `MAX_INTERVAL`. The threshold is a speed setting with its row in `docs/numbers.md` and its ×2/÷2 test, in C1's step | One place for sleep (`CLAUDE.md` 2.1); a second mechanism would be a second idea of the same thing | A sleep list of its own in `Life` |
| S28 | C2 | C2 starts by measuring where the pace error comes from; the method is chosen after that measure. If it is growth through the capture area, the survey's set-aside projects each node's area over its interval. The method is called a closer approximation, with its error measured at 36, 72 and 144 days, not an exact solution | No method is known to be right before the error is located; a closed form for a non-linear node is not exact | Pick the integrator first |
| S29 | B1 | B1 is for nodes without a brain only: one store per node (the parts share the node's stores, heat and brood) and parts that differ only in members and sizes; nodes with a brain keep their young nodes. Before building, a Haiku agent measures on both benches the share of bodies that are young nodes without a brain, the share of updates they take, and how many have no adult node of their kind in their cell; B1's target is set from that | One store makes births and maturing inside a node move no matter, so they cannot break the books; animals' decisions do not change (rule 3 of plan 2.2); a measured target replaces guesses that did not agree | One node per kind for every node, brain or not |
| S30 | B2 | The plan no longer promises that "an individual that learned is never merged". Today's animal nodes are the handover's own population nodes (7.1) and keep merging as they do; when people-like minds come, "a mind that stays individual" is one test in `nodes::may_join` | Exempting every body whose rows moved from its born rows would change today's world (almost every animal node learns) and make it slower; the promise was wider than the owner's wish (that the door to people-like bodies stays open) | Exempt every learner from every merge now |
| S31 | E | E2 is "hot spots", built right after phase A from the baseline profile (`spread::part_acts` with `nodes::take_in` and `nodes::add`, `copy::select::sample`, `nodes::crowd`, one world `apply` per update, `pow`), each its own commit with its measured gain (kill line 2% of a bench). E1 (columns) is built only if a callgrind cache simulation shows the body update losing at least 10% to cache misses. E3 (the stage path for bodies without a brain) is built only if a profile after D2 shows a stage worth at least 10% of the bodies' time; no `std::simd`, no new crates, only auto-vectorisation; its gain is unknown | The profile is flat but names these spots; columns and stages pay only if the memory or the repeated work is really the cost; `choose::act` (19% on the globe) is not touched by E3 | Build E1 and the stage path as first planned |
| S32 | all | The order follows the measured shares: A1 (done), A2, A3, E2, then D3 as a parallel lane, B1, B3, B2, C2, C1, then D2 (branch), D1, then E1 and E3 (gated), then Z | Bodies are three quarters of a year; cheap measured wins first, the big refactor after the body code has settled | The first order (B before the hot spots, E last) |
| S33 | all | Each level-3 step that changes a law or a speed setting (B1, B3, B2, C2, C1, D2.2, D2.3, D2.4, E3) runs the key-number test once; steps merge one at a time and a step re-pins the golden test on the `main` it merges into; B3 and B2 run one after the other (both re-pin it) | A step merged on a stale golden would hide a change; the key numbers are the only check of a level-3 step | Key numbers only at the end of each phase |
| S34 | A2, A3 | `target-cpu=native` stays if it pays, though it makes fingerprints depend on the machine (handover 12.5 asks the same history only on the same machine and build). Inside one process rayon's pool is shared, so the seeds of `tape/tests/ranges.rs` do not oversubscribe the cores; A3 measures separate `ew` runs side by side with and without `RAYON_NUM_THREADS=1` and documents the better | Handover 12.5; a measure instead of a guess | Portable builds only |
| S35 | A2 | `codegen-units = 1` is kept. Fat LTO, `target-cpu=native`, `panic = "abort"` and PGO are not (and there is no `scripts/pgo.sh`). `.cargo/config.toml` is not made, so S34's machine-dependent fingerprints do not arise | Measured in `measures.md`: only `codegen-units = 1` gains over 2% on both benches. PGO gains 5% and 4% over it, under the 8% bar, for a second build and a script | Keep PGO with a 30-line `scripts/pgo.sh` (the flow is in `measures.md`) |
